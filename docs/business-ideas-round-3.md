# Round 3 — The Price Hypothesis, Tested

**Prepared:** 22 September 2026
**Question asked:** is there an Indian market for hanbok and other cultural dress at a *low* price point, sold domestically, startable on ₹1–2 lakh?

**Answer:** the price hypothesis is half right, and the surviving half is the more valuable half. But the product has to change.

---

## 0. What you got right

Three things, and they matter:

**1. The ₹12,000 listing never tested the market.** Verified: the cheapest actual hanbok garment on Amazon.in is **₹5,624**. Filter the whole catalogue under ₹3,000 and there is no hanbok garment at all — just Korean-style Western maxi dresses, a hanbok doll and hair accessories. Amazon.in's official Hanbok Store contains **three products**: ₹4,612, ₹22,903, ₹25,922. Flipkart has none.

**Nobody in India has ever offered a hanbok below ₹5,624.** The earlier "no demand" verdict was drawn from a price point at which demand could not exist. You were right to keep pushing on this.

**2. Price is genuinely a binding constraint in this category** — and there is a clean natural experiment proving it. See §1.

**3. "No occasion in India" was wrong.** I said that, and it doesn't hold. School International Day and fancy-dress competitions are real, recurring, and demonstrably convert — Indian-brand costumes at ₹498–1,199 carry Indian reviews dated **2 September 2026, 23 August 2026, 19 November 2025**. Themed and Halloween parties too. The occasions exist; I was looking for Korean festivals instead of Indian ones.

## 2. But the product is wrong, independently of price

This is the part that doesn't go your way, and the evidence is clean.

### The control group already ran your experiment

Cheongsam/qipao is the nearest neighbour to hanbok — same "East Asian traditional dress," same import channel, same marketplace. The difference is that sellers *have* pushed it to the bottom of the price ladder:

**A traditional Chinese dress has been available to Indian consumers at ₹545 and sold zero units.** The price was dropped 20× and demand did not appear. That is your experiment, run by someone else, with a null result. Hanbok is not different from cheongsam in any way that would reverse this.

### Five independent operators have already made this call

Every Indian business that follows the Korean-fandom audience with real money sells **contemporary Korean style or fandom merch — and none of them sells traditional dress**:

- **Morus Alba** — 35 dresses, ₹950–2,519, all contemporary
- **Blithester** — K-drama-inspired imported fashion, same pattern
- **KpopMerchIndia** — ₹870–3,680: albums, lightsticks, photocards, T-shirts, bomber jackets. No hanbok
- **KpopIndiaMerch** — no hanbok
- **Myntra and AJIO** — both run "Korean dress"/"Korean fashion" categories, entirely contemporary

These are the people closest to the Indian Korean-fandom wallet. They have all chosen not to carry hanbok. That is five independent operators converging.

## 3. Indian manufacturing — confirmed, and it solves the cost problem

This was the question I most wanted answered, and the answer is unambiguous: **yes, and it is already happening.**

- **Kaku Fancy Dresses**, Bhogal, Delhi, operating since 2006 — manufacturer and wholesaler of international traditional costumes, explicitly serving school competitions. **Already manufactures a "Korean Girl Costume" in India**, sizes 3-4y to 14-17y, ₹799–1,099 retail.
- **Indian cosplay manufacturing:** One Piece cosplay ₹499/piece (Pune), Itachi Naruto ₹780/piece (Delhi), adult costumes ₹1,600/piece.
- **Kimono-style garment manufacturing:** ₹100–4,999/piece. Bhalala Digital Print from ₹100/pc, Sudeep International ₹210/pc, English Creations Craze ₹297/pc, Fragrance Of India ₹4,999/pc with full customisation in silk/cotton/chiffon/rayon.

**A simplified hanbok-style jeogori + chima at ₹600–900 COGS in India is entirely achievable.** Your core premise survives: the duty wall is avoidable by not importing.

### What that does to the margin

Duty on the import routes runs ~40% effective on CIF, and India applies a **compound duty — the higher of ad valorem or a per-piece floor — specifically to stop cheap apparel imports.** That floor is what kills the cheap-import play.

| | China dropship | India bulk import | **India-manufactured** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landed cost | ₹2,184 | ₹1,040 | **₹750** |
| Contribution @ ₹1,499 | **negative** | ~₹99 | ~₹99 |
| Contribution @ ₹1,999 | **negative** | ~₹135 (7%) | **~₹425 (21%)** |
| Contribution @ ₹2,499 | **negative** | ~₹441 (18%) | **~₹741 (30%)** |

*After marketplace fees ~18%, fulfilment ~₹90, payment ~2%, and a returns allocation of ₹160–250/unit.*

**On your own price ladder: ₹1,499 is dead. ₹1,999 is breakeven before ad spend — and Amazon India CAC on a cold apparel SKU runs ₹150–400, which erases it. ₹2,499 is the real floor.**

China dropship cannot reach ₹1,999 legally at all. It only "works" via under-declaration or gift-declaration, which is the grey route Indian customs spent seven years closing. Don't build on it.

## 5. The honest reframe

What the evidence supports is not "hanbok, cheaper." It is:

> **India-manufactured K-pop-themed costume and fandom apparel at ₹1,999–2,499, sold into school-event and themed-party occasions.**

That business is live, growing, currently badly served (the market leader's ₹1,999 costume rates 2.5★), and riding **KPop Demon Hunters — 628M+ views, the most-watched Netflix film ever**, still 4th most-viewed Jan–Jun 2026, and it swept the 2025 Halloween costume charts.

It is a **costume and merch business, not a traditional dress business.** That distinction is the whole finding.

### Size it honestly

At Indian review rates of ~1–3% of units, the best-performing SKUs imply lifetime volumes in the **low thousands per SKU**, not tens of thousands. The verified event base is ~1,278 K-pop contest teams and ~2,000 Comic Con cosplayers nationally.

**Realistically ₹20–80 lakh/year revenue at ~25% contribution margin if executed well.** A viable small business. Not venture scale.

### The risk you must price in

This rides a **licensed IP wave**. Netflix and HYBE can and do enforce. Build the brand on *K-pop aesthetic and stage-costume styling*, never on character likenesses, official logos or title names. The moment you print "KPop Demon Hunters" on anything, you are one takedown from zero inventory value.

## 6. Where this leaves the two live options

Round 3 produced two businesses that clear your ₹30–50k/month bar. They are not the same business and you should pick one.

| | **A. K-pop / fandom costume** | **B. Classical dance costume** |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Live Indian reviews, Sept 2026, on a ₹1,999 Indian-made SKU | Verified trade ₹225–3,000 vs retail ₹8,200–32,000 |
| COGS → retail | ₹750 → ₹2,499 | ₹1,200–2,000 → ₹3,999–6,999 |
| Contribution | ~₹741 (30%) | ~₹2,100 (55%) |
| Repeat purchase | Weak — trend-driven | **Strong — children outgrow costumes** |
| CAC | ₹150–400 (marketplace) | **Near zero via dance-school referrals** |
| Returns risk | High (sizing, event deadlines) | Low *(reasoned, not measured)* |
| Durability | **Tied to an IP wave** | Structural — dance is permanent |
| Ceiling | ₹20–80 lakh/yr | Similar, but more defensible |

**My read: B is the better business, A is the better fit to your original instinct.** B has stronger unit economics, near-zero CAC, and doesn't depend on a Netflix release cycle. A is closer to what you actually wanted to build and has fresher demand evidence.

If you want to test your instinct rather than abandon it, **A is legitimately testable for ₹1.5 lakh.** One India-manufactured SKU at ₹2,499, 100 units, sold into school events and themed parties. That is a real test of a real hypothesis, not a leap of faith.

## 7. Do these first — in this order

**1. Close the Meesho gap.** This is the one finding that could still overturn everything. Meesho hard-blocked every automated attempt (Akamai 403 on web, API and proxy), and Meesho is exactly where a ₹500–1,500 garment lives. **Open the Meesho app on an Indian phone and search: hanbok, cheongsam, qipao, Korean traditional dress, kimono costume.** Record listing counts, prices, ratings, order counts. Your partner can do this in ten minutes. *If there is a thriving ₹800 hanbok market on Meesho, this entire verdict changes.*

**2. Get a real manufacturing quote.** Send a tech-pack (jeogori + chima, poly-satin, 3 sizes) to **Kaku Fancy Dresses**, **Fragrance Of India** and two Surat units. Ask 100/500/1000-piece pricing. One week, near-zero cost, and it converts the central cost assumption into a fact. Nobody has ever quoted an actual hanbok — the ₹600–900 figure is extrapolated from kimono and cosplay manufacturing.

**3. Search Instagram natively** for #hanbokindia, #koreandressindia, #hanbokdelhi, and check sellers in Imphal, Aizawl and Kohima. Automated search is US-geolocated and couldn't see this. A thriving informal channel would materially change the picture.

**4. Phone 3–5 clothing retailers in Imphal, Aizawl and Kohima.** Northeast India has documented deep Korean affinity — Arirang TV on Manipuri cable since 2008, government-run Indo-Korea festivals in Kohima. But the documented consumption is *contemporary* K-fashion, K-beauty and salons, and the wave was built on cheap pirated media, i.e. a price-sensitive, low-ARPU population. No hanbok retail was found anywhere in the region. Ask them directly what Korean product they stock and sell. **This is your best remaining geographic hypothesis.**

**5. Get actual category return rates** from Amazon Seller Central before trusting any ₹1,999 or ₹2,499 breakeven. Costume returns may run *higher* than general apparel because of sizing and event deadlines. The ₹160–250/unit returns allocation is a rule of thumb, not a measurement.

## 8. What I'd actually do

You've pushed on this four times, and each push produced better evidence than the last. That's the process working. Here's the state of it:

**Killed, with evidence:** hanbok as a product (₹545 cheongsam sold zero), dirndl (zero Amazon.in results), cheongsam, China dropship (arithmetically negative at every price point).

**Alive, with evidence:** India-manufactured K-pop costume at ₹2,499, and classical dance costumes.

**The pattern worth noticing:** across three rounds, every time the answer turned positive it was because the product was **made in India and sold to a buyer with a specific, recurring occasion.** Every time it turned negative it was because something was **imported into India for a buyer with no occasion.** Your idea kept improving as it moved along that axis — and the version that works is at the far end of it.

Steps 1 and 2 above cost nothing and take a week. Do them before committing a rupee. If Meesho is empty and the manufacturing quote comes back at ₹900, you have a real, tested, ₹1.5 lakh business to start — and it will be a better one than the idea you walked in with.
